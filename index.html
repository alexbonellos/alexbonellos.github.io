About

From not knowing what recruiting was to a summer at Fitch.

I'm Alex Bonellos, a finance student at the University of Arizona's Eller College of Management, graduating in May 2028.

When I started college, I didn't know what recruiting was or that it had timelines. I taught myself the process, passed the SIE, and reached out to people across the industry. That work led me to Fitch Ratings in New York for Summer 2026.

At Fitch I compiled deal research and peer ratings comparables, evaluated credit profiles and trends across six-plus sectors, and pitched unrated companies to the team as new rating opportunities.

Today I keep learning in public: I write a weekly fixed income market journal, and I co-founded Delta Capital Markets to teach other students capital markets basics. My goal is a career in credit and fixed income markets in New York.

Reasoning

Strongest match: growth. All 9 respondents chose Growth-Oriented, and it is one of my own seven words.

The gap that helps me: hard work. All 9 chose Hard-Working and Self-Confident. I chose neither. I treat hard work as the baseline, but other people see it as what defines me.

The words that don't carry over: Spontaneous (0 of 9) and Easygoing (1 of 9). That's the version of me friends see, not the one at work.

Why: my motivators explain it. Competition rates 85, business and ideas 80 each, and recognition only 10. I want to win and do good work, not be noticed doing it, so I underrate the effort other people notice.

Brand strategy

Lead with what others already confirm: hard work and growth. Then prove it with output instead of adjectives.

That's why this site points to things I do every week, like my market journal and my pitches, and why my video opens with "I work hard, I learn quickly, and I take feedback well."

For a credit team, that's the right promise from a junior: reliable work, with quality that improves every week.

Reasoning

Why the gap exists: the SPI measures how I'm wired, and the PRS measures how I come across. I think in tasks and bottom lines, but around people I'm more outgoing and assertive than I give myself credit for.

Where it costs me: formal presentations. When I script every word, the Driver takes over and I come across as stiff. A teammate flagged it after our first presentation, and I heard the same in class: I sounded scripted.

Brand strategy

Bottom line first, then talk, don't recite. I now prepare from short bullet points, not scripts, and open with the conclusion.

Fixed income needs both sides of this: the Driver gets the analysis right, and the Expressive side gets it across to a client or a portfolio manager. My video applies it: it opens with the bottom line.

Reasoning

The honest trend: my overall average fell from 3.94 in Round 1 to about the team average by Rounds 2 and 3, as projects got bigger and I took the lead on more of them.

Why: it's the Driver pattern my SPI warned about: start fast, structure everything, and leave less assertive teammates behind. Teammates valued the head start but wanted to hear the plan first. The quality of my work recovered in Round 3; how I communicate around the work is the gap still open.

Brand strategy

Brief before I build. A five-minute kickoff where I share the plan, my schedule and who owns what, then I start.

That's what a desk or a credit team wants from a junior: speed, without surprises. It's also what my Fitch manager meant by coachable. I take feedback and change how I work.
