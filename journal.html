Week of October 26 to 30, 2026

Written by Alex Bonellos. Compiled Nov 1, 2026. Figures dated as published.

I write one of these every Friday to practice thinking and talking about markets the way a fixed income desk does: rates and credit first, then currencies and equities, and every move explained as cause and effect. This is one full week, lightly trimmed.

The week in one paragraph

The Fed held but leaned hawkish, and the economy ran hot (Q3 GDP 3.4%). Even so, the 2-year fell 12bp to 4.56%, because the last two days were an AI selloff: Alphabet down about 13% Thursday, then Intel down 29% and Amazon's cash burn Friday. The Nasdaq lost 2.8% Monday to Friday. High yield widened 12bp on Thursday as the 2-year fell. That is the fear signal, not a Fed call. Long yields barely moved, so the curve steepened hard (2s30s up 12bp to 75bp).

1. Rates

The front end rallied, the long end didn't. 2-year down 12bp to 4.56%, 5-year down 13bp to 4.71%, 10-year down 6bp to 4.94%, 30-year unchanged at 5.31%. When short yields fall and long yields stay put, the curve steepens. Here it was a bull steepener: the market thinks the Fed may hike less, but still wants a high yield to hold 30-year paper.

  • Wed Oct 28 (Fed day): the Fed held 10 to 2, with two votes for a hike, and upgraded its growth wording to "a strong pace." Long yields rose on strong growth plus sticky inflation; 2s10s jumped from 29 to 38bp in one day.
  • Thu Oct 29: 2-year down 6bp to 4.61%, the biggest move of the week, and it ran against the data. Q3 GDP came in at 3.4% vs. 3.2% expected. Strong data normally pushes yields up. Instead, Alphabet fell about 13% after earnings and bonds were bought for safety.
  • Fri Oct 30: 2-year down 5bp to 4.56%. Intel fell 29% on a cash-burn warning, Amazon fell on its spending plans, and the S&P lost 1.8%. A second day of flight to safety.

The 2-year now sits 68bp above the effective fed funds rate (4.56% vs. 3.88%), down from 81bp last week. The market is still pricing hikes, just fewer.

2. Credit

IG OAS 90 to 89bp. HY OAS 330 to 326bp (Monday to Thursday). The HY path: 338, 330, 318, 314, 326.

What credit settles. Monday to Wednesday, high yield tightened 24bp from the prior Friday, a relief rally. Thursday it widened 12bp on the same day the 2-year fell 6bp despite strong GDP. If the 2-year had fallen because traders expected a softer Fed, high yield would have tightened. It widened. So Thursday's rally was fear.

Cross-check: rates, credit and equities all agree on Thursday and Friday. The one piece that doesn't fit is FX: the dollar and yen didn't catch a safe-haven bid, because their own central banks (ECB, BoJ) were driving them.

3. Currencies

  • Dollar flat (DXY 101.96 to 101.88). A risk-off week usually lifts the dollar; this one didn't, because US yields fell.
  • EUR/USD down 0.13% to 1.1160. The ECB held at 2.50% on Oct 29 and said a hike was debated.
  • USD/JPY flat at 157.55. The Bank of Japan held at 1.25%, 8 to 1, with one vote for a hike.
  • Oil: WTI $104.12, Brent $117.64 on Friday. Still above $100, still the inflation problem.

4. Themes I'm watching

  • Strong economy, nervous markets. GDP 3.4%, a Fed calling growth "strong," oil above $100. Yet the front end rallied on stock-market fear, not data.
  • AI spending is now a credit question. When capex exceeds operating cash flow, the gap is debt, and that debt prices off a 5% long end. Amazon, Alphabet and Intel all sold off this week on spending or cash flow.
  • Global central banks on hold but leaning to hike. Fed (two hike dissents), ECB (hike debated), BoJ (one hike vote). December is the month for all three.
  • For fixed income desks: a steeper curve and volatile credit are good for trading activity.
